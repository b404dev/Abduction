# UFO/UAP Research and Splash Copy

This note records the sources and editorial decisions behind Abduction's encounter-themed splash copy. It is a product research document, not an assertion that any reported object was extraterrestrial.

## Editorial standard

- Treat **unidentified** as a case status, not an origin.
- Distinguish direct testimony, contemporaneous records, later recollections, and unsupported claims.
- Preserve the witness's meaning when shortening testimony.
- Display the witness, role, event, date, and source type with every quotation.
- Prefer government archives, sworn testimony, and contemporaneous documents over retellings.
- Label disputed or unauthenticated programs as claims rather than established history.

## Historical timeline

### 1940s - the modern UFO question enters government files

**1946-1947 - Project Saucer**

The U.S. military began organizing reports of unusual aerial objects amid concern that unfamiliar sightings could represent foreign technology. AARO's historical review uses Project Saucer for the activity immediately preceding Project Sign.

**24 June 1947 - Kenneth Arnold sighting**

Civilian pilot Kenneth Arnold reported seeing nine fast-moving objects near Mount Rainier. Newspaper descriptions popularized the phrase "flying saucer," helping create the language and public interest surrounding later investigations.

**July 1947 - Roswell**

Personnel recovered balloon-related debris near Roswell, New Mexico. The event later became associated with an alleged crashed spacecraft. Official Air Force reviews concluded that the material was consistent with the then-classified Project Mogul balloon program; the available government record does not establish recovery of alien bodies or technology.

**December 1947-January 1948 - Project Sign begins**

The Air Force created Project Sign, initially called Project Saucer, to collect, collate, evaluate, and distribute sighting information. The concern was broader than extraterrestrial visitation: investigators also considered foreign weapons, misidentification, atmospheric effects, and flight safety.

**1948 - the alleged Estimate of the Situation**

Project Sign personnel were later said to have drafted an assessment arguing that some reports might be interplanetary. AARO notes that leadership rejected it for lack of proof and that the report was never published. It should therefore be described as a reported internal assessment, not a surviving declassified conclusion.

**February 1949 - Project Grudge replaces Sign**

Grudge took a more skeptical and reduced approach. Its investigators attributed reports to balloons, aircraft, astronomical objects, optical effects, and other conventional causes. The Air Force announced its termination in December 1949, although report handling continued.

### 1950s - radar incidents, intelligence concern, and Blue Book

**1950-1952 - Project Magnet in Canada**

Transport Canada engineer Wilbert B. Smith led Project Magnet, which considered whether reported objects might reveal useful scientific principles, including possible propulsion concepts. It did not establish the existence of extraterrestrial vehicles.

**1952-1954 - Canada's Project Second Storey**

Canada's Defence Research Board convened a committee to assess "flying saucer" reports and create a standardized reporting form. After six meetings, the committee concluded that the reports did not present a security threat or sufficient scientific interest to justify continued military investigation.

**March 1952 - Project Blue Book receives its name**

The Air Force reorganized its investigation as Project Blue Book at Wright-Patterson Air Force Base. It became the best-known and longest-running U.S. government UFO investigation.

**19-20 and 26-27 July 1952 - Washington radar incidents**

Radar operators around Washington National Airport and Andrews Air Force Base observed unexplained returns. Interceptors were launched but did not establish the identity of extraordinary craft. Temperature inversions and associated radar propagation effects were offered as an explanation. The incidents generated national headlines and increased White House and CIA interest.

**Late 1952 - CIA special study group**

The CIA's Office of Scientific Intelligence and Office of Current Intelligence reviewed Air Force data. Officials were concerned about technological surprise, overloaded warning channels, and possible exploitation of public anxiety as much as the reported objects themselves.

**January 1953 - Robertson Panel**

A CIA-sponsored panel of scientists reviewed selected reports. It did not find evidence of an extraterrestrial threat and emphasized the risk that mass reporting could overwhelm defense communications or be exploited during a crisis.

**1953-1956 - expanded military reporting**

Blue Book worked alongside Air Force intelligence and the 4602d Air Intelligence Service Squadron. National Archives holdings include case files, administrative material, Office of Special Investigations records, photographs, film, and overseas reporting.

### 1960s - durable cases and the end of Blue Book

**24 April 1964 - Socorro, New Mexico**

Police officer Lonnie Zamora reported seeing an egg- or oval-shaped object near the ground, two figures nearby, and the object's rapid departure. Investigators documented ground impressions and scorched vegetation. Blue Book retained the case as unidentified; that classification does not determine the object's origin.

**1966-1968 - University of Colorado study**

The Air Force funded an independent study led by physicist Edward Condon. Its final report concluded that further extensive UFO study was unlikely to produce major scientific discoveries, while individual cases within the report continued to attract debate.

**17 December 1969 - Blue Book termination announced**

The Air Force closed Blue Book after reviewing its own experience, the Colorado study, and a National Academy of Sciences review. Across the program's historical records, 12,618 reports were logged and 701 remained classified as unidentified.

### 1970s and 1980s - international archives and military witnesses

**1976 - Blue Book records become publicly available**

After personally identifying information was redacted, Blue Book material was opened for public research. The collection is now available through National Archives microfilm and large digital bulk downloads.

**1977 - France establishes GEPAN**

The French space agency CNES established a dedicated group to collect and scientifically investigate unusual aerospace observations. The organization later operated as SEPRA and then GEIPAN, which continues publishing anonymized reports and case assessments.

**18-19 September 1976 - Tehran encounter**

Iranian military personnel reported visual and instrument observations during an attempted interception near Tehran. A U.S. Defense Intelligence Agency information report documented the account. The report records the incident; it does not by itself validate an extraterrestrial explanation.

**26-28 December 1980 - Rendlesham Forest**

U.S. Air Force security personnel near RAF Woodbridge reported unusual lights in the forest. Lt. Col. Charles Halt later recorded observations and submitted the "Unexplained Lights" memorandum. The surviving record makes this historically important, while proposed explanations and later recollections remain disputed.

### 1990s and 2000s - releases, reviews, and renewed defense work

**1994-1997 - official Roswell reviews**

At the request of the U.S. Congress, the Air Force and Government Accountability Office searched for relevant records. Air Force reports connected the debris story to Project Mogul and argued that later body accounts had become conflated with test-dummy recoveries and unrelated accidents.

**1997 - the UK's public UFO hotline**

The Ministry of Defence operated a public reporting line as part of its UFO desk. Its purpose was to determine whether reports had defense significance, not to investigate extraterrestrial life.

**1997-2000 - Project Condign**

The UK Ministry of Defence commissioned a classified study of reports received by its UFO desk. Released later under freedom-of-information rules, it concluded that reported phenomena did not present a significant defense threat and discussed possible atmospheric explanations.

**2007/2008-2012 - AAWSAP**

The U.S. Defense Intelligence Agency funded the Advanced Aerospace Weapon System Applications Program. AARO's historical review distinguishes the documented contract program from later claims about a broader or hidden extraterrestrial-material effort.

**2009 - the UK closes its UFO desk**

The Ministry of Defence ended its reporting hotline and dedicated UFO function, concluding that the work did not provide defense value. Surviving files were progressively transferred to the UK National Archives.

### 2010s and 2020s - UAP replaces UFO in official language

**14 November 2004 / publicly discussed from 2017 - USS Nimitz encounter**

Navy aviators including David Fravor reported encountering a white oblong object during training off Southern California. Fravor later described the event in sworn congressional testimony. Department of Defense videos associated with Navy encounters were officially released in 2020, but imagery alone does not establish origin.

**2014-2015 - U.S. East Coast aviator reports**

Ryan Graves and other Navy personnel reported repeated radar and sensor tracks in training airspace. Their accounts helped reframe UAP as a flight-safety, sensor-analysis, and reporting issue.

**August 2020 - UAP Task Force**

The Department of Defense formally established the Navy-led UAP Task Force to detect, analyze, and catalog objects that might threaten flight safety or national security.

**November 2021 - AOIMSG**

The Airborne Object Identification and Management Synchronization Group succeeded the UAP Task Force, broadening coordination across the Department of Defense and intelligence community.

**July 2022 - AARO**

The Department of Defense established the All-domain Anomaly Resolution Office. Its remit covers anomalous detections across air, sea, space, and transmedium environments, with emphasis on resolving cases using sufficient, verifiable data.

**26 July 2023 - congressional witness hearing**

David Fravor and Ryan Graves gave public testimony about their encounter experiences and aviation-safety concerns. David Grusch testified about alleged programs based largely on information he said others had provided; those allegations should be separated from firsthand encounter testimony.

**March 2024 - AARO Historical Record Report, Volume I**

AARO published a review of U.S. government involvement from 1945 through October 2023. It reported no verified evidence that the U.S. government or private industry possessed extraterrestrial technology, while acknowledging that cases can remain unresolved when usable data are absent.

**2025 - Canada's Sky Canada report**

Canada's Office of the Chief Science Advisor published a review of how public UAP reports have been handled. It documented the history of Project Magnet, Project Second Storey, and the later transfer of reports to the National Research Council.

## Documented investigation programs

### United States

- **Project Saucer / Project Sign (1947-1949):** the first dedicated U.S. Air Force effort to collect and evaluate reports.
- **Project Grudge (1949):** a reduced continuation of Sign that generally attributed reports to ordinary phenomena.
- **Project Blue Book (1952-1969):** received 12,618 reports; the Air Force listed 701 as unidentified when the program closed.
- **CIA study group and Robertson Panel (1952-1953):** examined possible national-security, air-defense, and public-reaction implications.
- **AAWSAP / AATIP (2009-2012):** modern defense work later reviewed in AARO's historical report.
- **UAP Task Force (2020), AOIMSG (2021), and AARO (2022-present):** successive efforts focused on detection, attribution, flight safety, and national security.

### United Kingdom

The Ministry of Defence collected reports for decades and closed its UFO desk and reporting hotline in 2009. Surviving files cover policy, Parliamentary business, public correspondence, military reports, and cases such as Rendlesham Forest. Many early records are unavailable because UFO files were routinely destroyed before the retention policy changed.

- [UK National Archives UFO research guide](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/ufos/)
- [UK National Archives UFO reports](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/explore-the-collection/explore-by-time-period/postwar/ufo-reports/)

### Canada

- **Project Magnet:** investigated whether reported phenomena might have scientific or propulsion significance.
- **Project Second Storey:** a Defence Research Board committee that standardized reporting and assessed security implications.
- Responsibility passed to the National Research Council in 1967, alongside declassification of the two projects.

Reference: [Government of Canada - Management of Public UAP Reporting](https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/science/en/office-chief-science-advisor/sky-canada-project/management-public-reporting-unidentified-aerial-phenomena-canada)

### France

CNES created GEPAN in 1977; the work continued through SEPRA and GEIPAN. GEIPAN collects testimony, investigates using recognized scientific knowledge, and publishes anonymized reports. It explicitly does not equate UAP study with extraterrestrial research.

Reference: [CNES-GEIPAN mission and methods](https://www.cnes-geipan.fr/en/missions-methodes-et-resultats)

## Encounter testimony selected for the splash

### David Fravor - USS Nimitz, 14 November 2004

Fravor testified that four aviators observed a white, oblong object moving abruptly above disturbed water. He reported that it reacted to his aircraft, accelerated away, and disappeared from visual contact.

Splash excerpt:

> "All four of us looked down and saw a white Tic Tac object moving very abruptly over the water. As we pulled nose onto it, it rapidly accelerated in front of us and disappeared."

Source: [2023 House UAP hearing transcript](https://www.congress.gov/118/meeting/house/116282/documents/HHRG-118-GO06-Transcript-20230726.pdf)

### Ryan Graves - East Coast training ranges, 2014-2015

Graves testified that upgraded radar initially produced tracks pilots suspected were software errors. He said infrared sensors and aircrew observations later corroborated the presence of physical objects. His testimony frames the encounters primarily as an aviation-safety and reporting problem.

Splash excerpt:

> "We began to detect unknown objects in our airspace. Initially dismissed as software glitches, we soon corroborated these radar tracks with infrared sensors, confirming their physical presence."

Source: [Ryan Graves's written congressional testimony](https://www.congress.gov/118/meeting/house/116282/witnesses/HHRG-118-GO06-Wstate-GravesR-20230726.pdf)

### Charles Halt - Rendlesham Forest, December 1980

Halt's contemporaneous memorandum recorded reports of unusual lights, a metallic triangular appearance, movement through trees, ground depressions, and later pulsing lights. The memo documents what personnel reported; it does not establish an extraterrestrial explanation.

Memorable recorded phrases include "There's something very, very strange" and "This is unreal." These are not currently in the splash rotation.

## Product implementation

The splash chooses one encounter excerpt randomly on each application launch. Each card includes:

- the contextual excerpt;
- witness name and service;
- event location and date;
- source type.

The splash runs in two stages: a three-second ship animation followed by the encounter card. Pressing Enter or clicking the start control continues immediately; otherwise the app starts automatically after a five-second reading period. Responsive rules reduce the quote size and hide secondary status copy on short displays.

Implementation:

- `frontend/src/components/Splash.tsx`: encounter data, random selection, attribution markup, and splash duration.
- `frontend/src/App.tsx`: splash lifecycle and the six-hour repeat-visit cooldown.
- `frontend/src/cockpit.css`: quote-card layout and responsive presentation.

## Claims requiring special treatment

Roswell and MJ-12 should not be presented as confirmed alien-recovery programs. The National Archives reports that its searches did not authenticate the alleged MJ-12 briefing material. Official Roswell reviews attributed recovered material to the then-classified Project Mogul balloon program and did not find records of recovered extraterrestrial bodies or technology.

For future content, use labels such as **official record**, **sworn testimony**, **unresolved**, **explained**, **insufficient data**, **disputed**, and **claim without documentary support**.
